### Assessment of the growth case
The growth advocate’s 10% recommendation is predicated on the assumption that a Forward PE of 14.41 represents a simple market oversight. This is a dangerous simplification. The market is not "missing" the earnings; it is heavily discounting them due to the quality of those earnings. By ignoring that Accounts Receivable ($63.06B) now significantly outweighs the quarterly Operating Cash Flow ($24.08B), the advocate assumes this credit-fueled expansion is risk-free. It is not. Betting 10% of a portfolio on a company where cash conversion is lagging behind revenue growth is essentially gambling on the solvency of the company's customers, not just the demand for the hardware.

### Assessment of the capital case
The capital steward’s 2% proposal effectively treats NVDA as a distressed asset. This ignores the reality of the balance sheet. While the steward correctly highlights the OCF drop from $50.34B to $24.08B, they gloss over the $62.47B cash pile. A firm with $62.47B in liquid assets is not in an immediate liquidity crisis. The steward’s fear of a "working capital crisis" assumes imminent, catastrophic impairment of these receivables, which is not supported by current data. A 2% allocation is too defensive; it treats a profitability juggernaut with nearly $60B in quarterly net income as a high-risk, speculative trade rather than a core, albeit volatile, holding.

### Recommendation
A 3% allocation is the only size supported by the evidence. This recognizes that NVDA is currently a liquidity powerhouse capable of weathering temporary working capital mismatches, justifying more than the steward's 2% defensive stance. However, the structural rise in receivables relative to cash flow prevents the 10% aggression of the growth advocate. A 3% allocation captures the upside of the AI infrastructure cycle while capping exposure to a level that accounts for the legitimate credit risk inherent in the current revenue model.

### What would change my mind
I will move to a 5–6% allocation if the next quarterly report shows Accounts Receivable flattening while Operating Cash Flow recovers toward the $40B+ level, confirming efficient cash conversion. I will reduce to a <1% position if the receivables-to-revenue ratio continues to expand for two consecutive quarters, signaling that the growth is increasingly driven by artificial credit extension rather than organic demand.
