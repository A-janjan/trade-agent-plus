## Market Cap

definition of market cap: Market Cap=Share Price × Number of Outstanding Shares

## P / E

P/E -> Price-to-Earnings ratio

P/E = [Earnings Per Share (EPS)] / [Share Price]
or
P/E = [Market Cap​] / [Net Income]

### What does a high/low P/E mean?

High P/E → investors are paying more for each dollar of current earnings. This can happen when the market expects higher future growth, though it can also reflect other factors.


Low P/E → investors are paying less per dollar of current earnings. This can reflect lower expected growth, higher perceived risk, cyclical earnings, or other factors.

### EPS

EPS = Earnings Per Share.

EPS=
(Weighted Average Shares Outstanding − Preferred Dividends) / Net Income ≈ (Shares Outstanding) / (Net Income)


## EBITDA

> Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization.

EBITDA = Net Income + Interest + Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization = Revenue - Operating Expenses excluding D&A 

hint: D&A -> Depreciation & Amortization

## Net Income

Net Income = Revenue - expenses


### The important hierarchy

Revenue

↓

− Cost of goods sold (COGS) [direct cost of producing or acquiring the products/services that a company actually sold]

↓

Gross profit

↓

− Operating expenses

↓

Operating income (EBIT)

↓

− Interest + other expenses

↓

− Taxes

↓

Net income

## Equity

the value that belongs to the owners/shareholders after subtracting what the company owes.

> What would be left for the owners if the company sold everything and paid all its debts?

$$ \boxed{\text{Equity} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}} $$


Liabilities = what the company owes

## ROE = Return on Equity

How much profit does the company generate from shareholders' money?

ROE = (Net Income​​)/(Shareholders Equity)

> One important caveat: higher ROE isn't automatically better. A company can increase ROE substantially by using more debt, which also increases financial risk.


## ROA = Return on Assets

How much profit does the company generate from all the assets it controls?

ROA = (Net Income) / (Total Assets)


## Free cash flow

> "After running the business and buying necessary equipment/assets, how much actual cash is left?"

$$ \boxed{FCF = Operating\ Cash\ Flow - Capital\ Expenditures} $$

Where:

Operating Cash Flow (OCF) = cash generated by the company's normal business

Capital Expenditures (CapEx) = money spent buying/maintaining long-term assets such as equipment, buildings, servers, etc.

## P/E (TTM)

> Price-to-Earnings ratio based on the Trailing Twelve Months.

> How much are investors currently paying for $1 of the company's earnings over the most recent 12 months?

$$ \boxed{P/E_{TTM}=\frac{\text{Current Share Price}}{\text{EPS}_{TTM}}} $$

where TTM EPS is the company's earnings per share from the latest 12 months.

## Forward PE

Forward P/E (Forward Price-to-Earnings ratio) estimates how expensive a stock is relative to its expected future earnings.

$$ \boxed{\text{Forward P/E}= \frac{\text{Current Share Price}} {\text{Expected EPS}}} $$


## PEG

PEG = Price/Earnings-to-Growth ratio.

It adjusts the P/E ratio for expected earnings growth.

> P/E tells you how expensive a company is relative to its earnings. PEG asks whether that price is reasonable given the company's growth rate.

$$ \boxed{PEG = \frac{P/E}{\text{Expected Earnings Growth Rate}}} $$

Expected annual EPS growth

## Price-to-Book ratio

It compares a company's market value to the accounting value of its equity.

Formula
$$ \boxed{P/B = \frac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Book Value per Share}}} $$

Or equivalently:

$$ \boxed{P/B = \frac{\text{Market Capitalization}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}} $$

## Profit Margin

Profit Margin tells you what percentage of a company's revenue becomes profit.


$$ \boxed{\text{Net Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Revenue}}\times100} $$


$$ \boxed{Revenue \rightarrow Gross\ Margin \rightarrow Operating\ Margin \rightarrow Net\ Margin} $$


## Operating Margin

Operating Margin tells you what percentage of a company's revenue remains after paying the costs of running its core business, but before interest and taxes.

$$ \boxed{\text{Operating Margin}= \frac{\text{Operating Income}}{\text{Revenue}}\times100} $$

And:

$$ \text{Operating Income} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS} - \text{Operating Expenses} $$

## Current Ratio

Current Ratio measures a company's ability to pay its short-term liabilities using its short-term assets.

$$ \boxed{\text{Current Ratio}= \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}} $$

"Current" generally means expected to be converted to cash or paid within about 1 year.


## Book Value

Book Value is the accounting value of a company's net assets—basically, what would theoretically remain for shareholders after subtracting all liabilities from all assets.

Formula
$$ \boxed{\text{Book Value} = \text{Total Assets} - \text{Total Liabilities}} $$

This is essentially the company's shareholders' equity.
